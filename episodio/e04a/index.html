The card beats the sentence

Speech is the weakest link in a loud bar: your pronunciation, plus their hearing, plus a room at full volume. A printed card removes all three, and it is the reason this part exists at all — where the stakes are highest we hand you the safest artefact rather than the cleverest sentence. Ask reception to write it, or carry your own: SOY ALÉRGICO/A A: … PUEDE SER GRAVE. Show it, do not perform it. Keep a photograph of it on your phone as well, because the paper one always ends up in the other jacket. In the kit, the same card lives behind the 🃏 button, so it is two taps away with the phone locked.

SOY ALÉRGICO A LOS FRUTOS SECOS. PUEDE SER GRAVE. — I am allergic to nuts. It can be serious. — the card, word for word
¿Se lo puede enseñar al cocinero, por favor? — seh loh PWEH-dheh en-seh-NYAR al koh-thee-NEH-roh — Could you show this to the chef, please?

The one place gender actually matters

Spanish gender is the most forgiving mistake in the language — say 'la problema' and your beer still arrives. There is exactly one place we do not let you relax, and it is here: the adjectives that describe YOU. A man is alérgico, celíaco, vegetariano, vegano; a woman is alérgica, celíaca, vegetariana, vegana. It matters not because it is grammar but because this is the sentence in which you are asking somebody to keep you out of hospital, and a clean, confident sentence is believed. The app already set your ending from your onboarding answer, and you will hear it used consistently from here on. You never choose it in the moment.

Soy alérgica al marisco. — soy ah-LEHR-khee-kah al mah-REES-koh — woman speaking
Soy alérgico al marisco. — soy ah-LEHR-khee-koh al mah-REES-koh — man speaking

Why the allergen list has to exist

EU Regulation 1169/2011, brought into Spanish law by Royal Decree 126/2015, obliges anyone serving food that did not arrive in a sealed packet — restaurants, bars, bakeries, market stalls — to hold allergen information for every dish and to make it available to you. It can be a folder, a laminated sheet, a screen or a spoken answer from trained staff, but it has to be there and it has to be accurate. That is why «¿Tienen la carta de alérgenos?» outranks every clever sentence you could build: it stops being a conversation about a foreigner's Spanish and becomes a request they are required to answer. If a place bristles at the question, you have learned something useful about that kitchen for free. (Source: AESAN, Reg. (UE) 1169/2011 and RD 126/2015. Worth re-checking before 2028.)

¿Tienen la carta de alérgenos? — TYEH-nen lah KAR-tah deh ah-LEHR-kheh-nohs — Do you have the allergen list?
¿Me lo puede confirmar con la cocina? — meh loh PWEH-dheh kon-feer-MAR kon lah koh-THEE-nah — Can you confirm that with the kitchen?

The hiding places, ranked by how often they catch people

1. Ham and chorizo as seasoning: judías verdes, espinacas, lentejas, sopa de verduras, and most things described as 'de la abuela'. 2. Stock: arroces and paellas 'de verduras' built on fish or meat caldo. 3. The shared fryer: croquetas, calamares, patatas and pescado through one vat of oil. 4. Hidden gluten: croquetas, anything rebozado (battered), flour-thickened sauces, and beer. 5. Fish where you did not look: anchoa in tinned things and in sauces, atún as standard in ensaladilla rusa, and salsa marinera, which is shellfish. None of this is deception. It is a kitchen where pork and stock do the job that salt and a stock cube do at home.

¿La ensaladilla lleva atún? — lah en-sah-lah-DEE-yah YEH-bah ah-TOON — Does the potato salad have tuna in it?
¿Lleva leche? — YEH-bah LEH-cheh — Does it have milk in it? (same word, new ingredient)

«Vegetal» is a description, not a promise

The word on the menu describes the visible contents, not the recipe. Crema de verduras is frequently built on chicken stock and finished with jamón; a menu section headed VERDURAS means the vegetable is the star, not that no animal was involved. Fish is the sharpest edge of this: in ordinary Spanish usage carne means meat from land animals, so «no como carne» does not exclude anchovies, tuna or prawns, and a waiter who brings you fish after that sentence is not being careless. Say «ni pescado» every single time. The clean, unambiguous words are vegetariano/a and vegano/a — and vegano is well understood in cities, less so in small inland towns, where the full list (sin carne, sin pescado, sin huevo, sin leche) does the work instead.

Soy vegetariana: no como carne ni pescado. — soy beh-kheh-tah-RYAH-nah, noh KOH-moh KAR-neh nee pehs-KAH-dhoh
¿Tienen algo vegano? — TYEH-nen AHL-goh beh-GAH-noh — Do you have anything vegan?

Cross-contamination, and what is actually reasonable to ask

The shared fryer is real and worth asking about if your allergy is severe: «¿Lo fríen en la misma freidora que el pescado?» Same logic for the plancha, the grill: «¿Lo hacen en la misma plancha?» What is NOT worth asking about is olive oil — it is the cooking medium of the entire country, it is excellent, and asking for your food to be cooked in something else marks you as the difficult table for no benefit at all. Pick your battles: fryer yes, oil no. And note the shape of rung three — «¿Puede avisar en cocina?» — which is the same frame you already use for «¿me puede escribir?» and «¿me puede recomendar algo para…?». One frame, three completely different emergencies.

¿Lo hacen en la misma plancha que el pescado? — loh AH-then en lah MEES-mah PLAHN-chah keh el pehs-KAH-dhoh — Same grill as the fish?
¿Puede usar una sartén limpia, por favor? — PWEH-dheh oo-SAR OO-nah sar-TEN LEEM-pyah — Could you use a clean pan, please?

False friends that live in this exact conversation

preservativo = condom (preservatives are conservantes). excitado = sexually aroused (excited is emocionado, or tengo muchas ganas). constipado = having a cold, NOT constipated (that is estreñido — and you will need this one at the pharmacy, not the restaurant). molestar is a perfectly polite word meaning 'to bother', so do not avoid it. And en absoluto does not mean 'absolutely': it means 'absolutely not', so if the waiter asks whether you enjoyed it, en absoluto is a very cold thing to say by accident. Use ¡totalmente! or ¡desde luego!

¿Lleva conservantes? — YEH-bah kon-sehr-BAHN-tehs — Does it contain preservatives?
Estoy muy emocionado con el viaje. — ehs-TOY mwee eh-moh-thyoh-NAH-dhoh kon el BYAH-kheh — I'm very excited about the trip.
