Ask on the way in, not on the way out

Every request in this part is cheaper at check-in than at any point afterwards, and for the same reason: at half past two the desk still has the whole board in front of it. A quieter room, a higher floor, a late checkout and a held bag are all decisions somebody makes by looking at a screen, and by six in the evening the screen has filled up. This is also why the order of the sentence matters when something is wrong: say what is happening first ('hay mucho ruido') and what you want second ('¿tienen otra habitación?'). The desk is not deciding whether to believe you; it is deciding what it has free. 'Quería otra habitación, si es posible' and 'quería una habitación más tranquila, si es posible' are the same move: the opening word and the closing three never move, and only the thing in the middle changes — and 'si es posible' is not padding here, it is the phrase that turns a demand into a question somebody can say yes to.

¿A qué hora es la entrada / la salida? — lah en-TRAH-dhah · lah sah-LEE-dhah — check-in / check-out time. 'Check-in' and 'check-out' are understood everywhere too.
¿Puedo hacer el check-out más tarde? — PWEH-dhoh ah-THEHR el chek-AWT mahs TAR-deh — can I check out later? Asked on day one it is usually free; asked on the day it is a favour.

Why they want your e-mail and your home address

Spain's Royal Decree 933/2021 (BOE-A-2021-17461), compulsory since 2 December 2024, requires accommodation providers — and car hire companies — to record and report to the security forces: full name, passport or ID document, nationality, sex, date of birth, phone, e-mail, full home address, number of travellers and, where minors are involved, the relationship between them. That is why the form is long, why it is sometimes only in Spanish, and why the receptionist does not look apologetic about it. It is not negotiable and it is not aimed at you: no data, no room. Hand over the passport, let them scan it, and move on. The one place you will meet the same duty again is the hire car counter.

¿Necesita mi pasaporte? — neh-theh-SEE-tah mee pah-sah-POR-teh — do you need my passport?
Aquí tiene. ¿Necesita algo más? — ah-KEE TYEH-neh — here you are. Do you need anything else?

The tourist tax, and where it actually bites

It is regional, not national. Catalonia and the Balearics have charged it for years, and the Basque Country joined in 2026. In Barcelona the total for a five-star hotel reached €12 a night from 1 April 2026, with the municipal surcharge on a published schedule to keep climbing towards 2029 — our source gives that figure as a nightly total and does not say per person, so treat the per-person question as one to ask at the desk. In the Balearics the announced increase never came into force, so it remains roughly €1 to €4 per person per night by hotel category, plus VAT on the tax itself. You will also see the Canary Islands listed on some aggregator sites — that one is genuinely contested and our own research flags it as unconfirmed, so do not assume either way: ask at the desk. The tax is almost never inside the price you paid on a booking site, and it is often collected in cash on the way out.

¿La tasa turística está incluida? — lah TAH-sah too-REES-tee-kah — is the tourist tax included?
¿Se paga aparte? ¿En efectivo? — seh PAH-gah ah-PAR-teh — is it paid separately? In cash?

Everything else you can hang on 'no funciona'

The shape is [thing] + 'no funciona', and the thing always arrives wearing its article. Learn the article and the word together and you never have to think about gender again — say 'la problema' and you will still get exactly what you asked for; gender is the most forgiving mistake in Spanish. You will also meet the same phrase written on machines all over the country: 'NO FUNCIONA', 'FUERA DE SERVICIO' and 'AVERIADO' all mean out of order, on lifts, ticket machines and petrol pumps. And when the thing is not a machine, the sentence changes at the front instead of the end: 'no hay' — there isn't any. No hay agua caliente, no hay toallas, no hay papel. One more pair worth having: 'la entrada' and 'la salida' are check-in and check-out on a hotel form, and the way in and the way out on a door. At the airport only the second one carries over — 'SALIDAS' is departures, while arrivals is 'LLEGADAS', not 'entradas'.

La llave no funciona. / El ascensor no funciona. — lah YAH-beh · el ah-then-SOR — the key / the lift isn't working
No hay toallas. / No hay papel. — noh ai toh-AH-yahs · noh ai pah-PEL — there are no towels / there's no paper
