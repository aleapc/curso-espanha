Doors, buildings and the toilet

SALIDA is the way out and never a sale; the sale is REBAJAS, and the official seasons are January and July. ENTRADA is both the entrance and the admission ticket. EMPUJAR is push and TIRAR is pull — em-PU-jar carries the mnemonic, and between them they cause the daily doorway collision. The toilets are ASEOS, SERVICIOS or LAVABOS; ask for the baño and you will be understood and instantly placed. CABALLEROS and SEÑORAS are gents and ladies, and modern bars also use M/H or ELLOS/ELLAS. ABIERTO and CERRADO are open and closed. PLANTA BAJA, sometimes just PB or 0, is the ground floor, which matters most to Americans: the Spanish first floor is one flight up from the street, so a lift button marked 1 does not take you out. NO FUNCIONA, FUERA DE SERVICIO and AVERIADO all mean out of order. PROHIBIDO EL PASO and PRIVADO mean no entry. CUIDADO CON EL ESCALÓN is mind the step, and it is usually there because the step is a bad one. AGUA NO POTABLE is on square fountains and means exactly what it says.

TIRAR · EMPUJAR — tee-RAR · em-poo-KHAR — pull · push. The one that looks like 'tear' is the one that means pull.
PLANTA BAJA — PLAN-tah BAH-khah — ground floor. The button you want in the lift is often 0 or PB, not 1.

Platforms, tracks and the door that does not open itself

ANDÉN is the platform on the metro; VÍA is the track number on Renfe. They are the same thing from where you are standing, and only one of them will be on the board in front of you. LLEGADAS and SALIDAS are arrivals and departures — the plural of the word that means 'exit' everywhere else in the country, which is the single most reliable false friend in Spain. CERCANÍAS, called RODALIES in Catalonia, is the commuter rail: a separate system with separate tickets, and the airport supplement is separate again. CORRESPONDENCIA and TRANSBORDO both mean interchange. TAQUILLA is the ticket office, VALIDAR is validate and RECARGA is top up. Two that are buttons rather than information: SOLICITE PARADA on a bus means request stop, and if you do not press it the bus does not stop; PULSE PARA ABRIR on a train means press to open, because on many Cercanías and older metro trains the door genuinely will not open by itself and people have watched their station go past waiting for it.

ANDÉN · VÍA — an-DEN · BEE-ah — platform (metro) · track (Renfe).
PULSE PARA ABRIR — POOL-seh PAH-rah ah-BREER — press to open. The door is waiting for you, not the other way round.

Money, the counter and the poster nobody reads

SOLO EFECTIVO means cash only, and it is entirely legal: there is no obligation in Spain to accept a card, which is why the cash machine is part of the first hour of the trip. On the card reader itself, CON CONVERSIÓN and SIN CONVERSIÓN — or a screen offering you the total in pounds or dollars instead of euros — are the same decision wearing two coats, and the option that shows you your own money is the expensive one. That gets a part of its own later, with the numbers attached. COCINA CERRADA means the kitchen has shut for the gap between services, and drinks carry on. S.M. or SEGÚN MERCADO next to shellfish and fish means the price is by weight at today's market rate, so the question to ask is the price per kilo and the weight of the piece, before it is cooked. MENÚ DEL DÍA is the weekday set lunch; PLATO DEL DÍA is one dish and not the same offer. And HAY HOJAS DE RECLAMACIONES A DISPOSICIÓN DEL CONSUMIDOR is the small framed poster you have walked past a hundred times: the complaint forms are official, they come in three copies, one of which goes to the regional consumer authority, and a business displaying that poster cannot then tell you it does not have any. Refusing to hand one over is itself an offence, separate from whatever the argument was about.

SOLO EFECTIVO — SOH-loh eh-fek-TEE-boh — cash only. Legal, common, and the reason to know where the cash machine is.
S.M. / SEGÚN MERCADO — seh-GOON mehr-KAH-dhoh — market price, by weight. Ask price per kilo and weight before you order.

The clock on the shutter: HORARIO, the middle of the day, and August

A HORARIO card usually has two ranges on it and not one, because the small shop closes in the middle of the day — roughly ten until half one or two, then again from half four or five until eight or half eight. What does not close: the big chains and supermarkets, the restaurants, which are at their busiest then, and the museums. HORARIO DE VERANO is the summer version of the same card, and where a second card is taped over the first, the season written on it is the one that applies. CERRADO POR VACACIONES means closed for the holidays and in practice means August: a family business shuts for the whole month, the online listing very often still says open, and the honest action is to ring before you walk twenty-five minutes across a city in thirty-four degrees. CERRADO POR DESCANSO DEL PERSONAL is the weekly rest day, usually Sunday or Monday. And FARMACIA DE GUARDIA is the one that saves a night: every locality always has at least one pharmacy on duty outside shop hours, and the rota is posted on the door of every pharmacy — including the dark, shut one you are standing in front of at half eleven at night. The word to look for is guardia.

CERRADO POR VACACIONES — theh-RRAH-dhoh por bah-kah-THYOH-nehs — closed for the holidays. It is August, not a closure.
FARMACIA DE GUARDIA — far-MAH-thyah deh GWAR-dhyah — duty pharmacy. The list of tonight's is taped to the door of the shut one.

ZBE: the plate on the road that photographs you

Since 1 January 2026 every Spanish municipality over fifty thousand inhabitants, plus the island territories and smaller towns over the pollution limits, has been required to run a low-emission zone — a zona de bajas emisiones, ZBE. They already cover a large share of the population and are still spreading, and they sit exactly where the historic centre is, which is exactly where the hotel is. Enforcement is an automatic camera that reads and traces foreign plates; the fine is classed as a serious infringement, up to €200, reduced to €100 for early payment, with no licence points. The structural trap is the sticker: the DGT environmental label is only issued to vehicles registered in Spain, so a British-plated car cannot simply buy one — a driver bringing their own car down through France has to register it online with each individual city before entering, which is usually free. A car hired in Spain has Spanish plates and a sticker already, so the question there is not whether it has one but which category it is, and that is a thirty-second question to ask at the counter while the person is still in front of you. Either way, the sign on the road is the last chance to decide: see ZBE, stop before it, leave the car outside and walk in.

ZBE · ACCESO RESTRINGIDO · CÁMARAS — Low-emission zone · restricted access · cameras. Do not drive in to check.
¿Puedo aparcar aquí? — PWEH-dhoh ah-par-KAR ah-KEE — Can I leave the car here? Asked outside the zone, not inside it.

When the sign is not in Spanish at all

In a neighbourhood bar in Barcelona, Girona, València, Palma, Bilbao or Santiago the menu and the sign may be in the co-official language only, and there is nothing to do about that except recognise a handful of words. Catalan: obert and tancat for open and closed, sortida for the way out, entrada, lavabos or serveis for the toilets, estirar and empènyer for pull and push, esmorzar, dinar and sopar for breakfast, lunch and dinner, preu for price, pa for bread and aigua for water. Basque: irteera for the way out, komuna for the toilet, itxita for closed. Galician: saída, aberto, pechado. You do not need to produce a single one of them, and nobody expects you to. There is a whole part later on what those languages are, what they are not, and the half-dozen courtesy words that visibly change how you are served.

OBERT · TANCAT — oh-BEHRT · tan-KAT — Catalan for open and closed, respelled the way the Spanish voice reads it out in this part.
ESTIRAR · EMPÈNYER — Catalan for pull and push. Same doorway, different letters — and these two you only ever read, so there is nothing to imitate.
