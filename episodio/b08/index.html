Why the yes-or-no question is worth more than the good one

A question is only as useful as the answer you can hold in your head. ¿Dónde está…? is a better sentence and a worse tool: it invites a route, and a route said at speed by someone who lives there is four instructions long. ¿Hay… por aquí? invites a yes, a no, or a pointed arm — all three of which you can act on immediately. Ask the closed question first, get the direction of travel, and start walking; ask the open one only when you already know the thing exists. This is the same trick the course uses everywhere: shrink the answer space before you ask.

¿Hay un cajero por aquí? — Is there a cash machine round here?
¿Dónde está la salida? — Where's the way out?

el / la or un / una — the one word that changes the question

¿Dónde está el baño? asks for the one this building has. ¿Dónde hay un baño? asks for any one at all, anywhere near here. Get it the wrong way round and nothing terrible happens — you will be pointed at something — but the second version is the one that works in the street, and the first is the one that works indoors. Learn each place name with its little word already glued to the front (el baño, la salida, los aseos, una farmacia, un cajero) and never think about it again.

¿Dónde están los aseos? — Where are the toilets? (in this restaurant)
¿Dónde hay una farmacia? — Where's there a chemist? (anywhere near)

The hatch: far, map, paper, finger

The repair kit from part three does its heaviest work in the street, and the order matters. ¿Está lejos? converts a paragraph into a number or a shrug. ¿Me lo puede enseñar en el mapa? moves the answer out of the air and onto something you can look at twice — a paper map on a bus shelter, a street plan on a wall, or your own screen. ¿Me lo escribe, por favor? gets the street name written down by someone who can spell it. And the finger is not a failure: pointing at the thing, the screen or the map is a legitimate, adult, universally understood move, and it is faster than the word you are hunting for.

¿Está lejos? — Is it far?
¿Me lo puede enseñar en el mapa? — Can you show me on the map?

Estoy perdido — and why it works

Estoy perdido, or perdida, is not a confession, it is a request that people respond to. It moves you from a person asking a question to a person who needs a hand, and it usually produces a slower, shorter, better answer than the question you were about to ask. It is also one of the handful of words in this course where the ending matters, because the word is describing you: -o if you are a man, -a if you are a woman. Everywhere else in Spanish you can get the ending wrong all day and still be understood.

Estoy perdido. / Estoy perdida. — I'm lost. (man / woman speaking)
Perdone, ¿está lejos la Plaza Mayor? — Excuse me, is the Plaza Mayor far?
