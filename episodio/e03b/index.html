Why 'quería' and not 'quiero' or 'me gustaría'

All three are understood. 'Quiero' is fine and slightly blunt; 'quería' is what you actually hear across a Spanish counter, and it costs you no extra syllables. 'Me gustaría' is the one your schoolbook taught and the one we have deliberately left out: it is padding, it takes longer to say, and the British habit of stacking padding in front of a request is exactly what stops you being served. The useful thing about 'quería' is not politeness at all — it is that it says nothing about the person you are talking to. You never have to decide whether this is a 'tú' situation or an 'usted' one, and that decision is the one an English speaker gets wrong under pressure.

Quería un adaptador de enchufe. — keh-REE-ah oon ah-dhap-tah-DOR deh en-CHOO-feh — I'd like a plug adaptor. (a word you have never said in your life, in a sentence you already own)
Quería dos billetes para Sevilla. — keh-REE-ah dohs bee-YEH-tehs PAH-rah seh-BEE-yah — I'd like two tickets to Seville. (same word, ticket office)

How far 'sin', 'con' and 'para' actually stretch

Two words, no verb, and they work as complete utterances: 'Sin hielo, por favor.' is a finished sentence. 'Sin' takes anything you want left out — gluten, gas, cebolla, hielo, conversión. 'Con' takes anything you want added — hielo, limón, leche. 'Para' is the widest of the three: it takes a purpose ('para llevar'), a number ('para dos'), a person ('para mí') and a document ('para el seguro' — the receipt for the insurance claim, which is the version of this frame you will be most grateful for). The one that pays: 'sin conversión' at a card machine. Under Regulation (EU) 2019/518, in force since April 2020, the machine has to disclose its markup over the European Central Bank rate before you accept, and the markups reported in 2026 sit at roughly ten to thirteen per cent. The rule of thumb is easier than the arithmetic: the option shown in your own currency is the wrong one.

¿Me lo pone para llevar? — meh loh POH-neh PAH-rah yeh-BAR — Could you put it up to take away? (the two frames stacked)
Quería la factura para el seguro. — keh-REE-ah lah fak-TOO-rah PAH-rah el seh-GOO-roh — I'd like the receipt for the insurance.

'La carta', 'el menú', and the four sizes

'El menú' is a set meal at a fixed price; 'la carta' is the list of everything the kitchen can make. Two more on the same page: 'entrada' is a starter, not the entrance, and 'tortilla' is a thick potato omelette, not a flatbread. On sizes: a 'tapa' is a small plate that comes with a drink; a 'pincho' or 'montadito' is the northern version on a slice of bread, paid by the unit; a 'ración' is a full sharing plate and genuinely large; a 'media ración' is half of one, which for two people is usually right. In the Basque Country the 'pintxo' system is different again: you help yourself from the bar top and the cocktail sticks on your plate are the bill, so you keep them. And 'S.M.' or 'según mercado' beside seafood means the price depends on weight and the market that morning — ask before you order.

¿Cuánto cuesta antes de pedirlo? — KWAN-toh KWEHS-tah AN-tehs deh peh-DEER-loh — How much is it before I order it?
¿Cuántos palillos tienes? — KWAN-tohs pah-LEE-yohs TYEH-nehs — 👂 'How many sticks have you got?' — he is not accusing you of anything, he is adding up.
