The passport: two dates, not one

For British passport holders travelling to Spain, the document must satisfy BOTH conditions at once: issued less than 10 years before the date you arrive, AND valid for at least 3 months after the day you leave the Schengen area. The reason so many people get caught is that passports issued between 2018 and 2020 could inherit up to 9 months from the previous document — so the expiry date printed inside can be perfectly fine while the issue date silently fails. You'll be refused at your departure airport, not at the Spanish border, which means the airline is under no obligation to refund anything. Check both dates today; renewals take weeks.

GHIC treats you. Insurance gets you home.

The UK GHIC gives British visitors medically necessary state healthcare in Spain on the same terms as a Spanish resident — emergency care, sudden illness, pre-existing conditions included. It does not cover repatriation, private hospitals or anything non-medical, and the UK government says explicitly that it is not a substitute for travel insurance. In the British-heavy stretches of the Costa del Sol, the clinic with the English sign is very often private, where the GHIC is worth nothing — which is why the single most valuable question in part 8A is '¿Esto es público o privado?'. American travellers have no equivalent card at all: there is no reciprocal health agreement between the US and Spain, and hospitals commonly ask non-residents to pay up front.

Roaming: check before you buy anything

British networks diverge sharply and change with each new plan generation, so the only reliable move is to open your own network's app and read your plan. As a rough map for 2026: O2 still includes EU roaming with no daily fee on most contract plans (fair use around 25 GB); Vodafone lets most plans use the UK allowance across Europe but applies a daily charge on many of them; EE sells a Roam Abroad daily pass; Three's Go Roam coverage varies by plan and has been revised. Daily charges apply automatically — there is no opt-out once you use the phone abroad. Two travellers × 14 days × £2/day ≈ £56, which is where a travel eSIM starts to make sense. If your network is O2, buy nothing.

Why they want the physical passport for a SIM card

Spanish prepaid SIMs must be registered to an identity document at the point of sale, and shops routinely refuse a photo or a scan on your phone — Vodafone and Movistar counters are reported to be the strictest. Take the passport itself. This is also the reason a travel eSIM bought and tested at home is the calmer option: it needs no counter, no queue and no document, but it does need an internet connection to activate, which you won't have until you're through the airport.

ETIAS: nothing to buy, nothing to do

ETIAS is the European travel authorisation that has been announced, priced at €20, and repeatedly postponed. As of July 2026 there is no open application portal, and press reports suggest a further slip into 2027 that the Commission has not confirmed. There is exactly one official website, run by the EU. Any site charging you a 'processing fee' for an ETIAS today is selling a document that does not yet exist. Check the official page about 60 days before you travel and take whatever it says then over whatever you read here.
