Taxi, ride-hailing, and why the rank is the plan

A taxi can be waved down in the street or taken from a rank — 'la parada de taxis'. A ride-hailing car (Uber, Cabify, Bolt) is legally a 'VTC' and can only be booked through its app; it cannot pick you up off the street. That is the whole reason anybody offering you a ride inside the arrivals hall is neither one nor the other. MADRID: a flat €33 between Barajas and any address inside the M-30, twenty-four hours a day, with no surcharges permitted on top; beyond the M-30 but within 9 km, Tariff 3 applies with a €22 initial charge. Madrid fares rose about 2.36% for 2026, and a reform in force from July 2026 adds anti-counterfeit vehicle stickers, accessibility requirements and a cap on surge pricing for ride-hailing. BARCELONA: an airport surcharge of €4.60, a minimum of about €21 on any journey leaving the airport, and a fixed €46 between the airport and the Moll Adossat cruise terminal; fares up about 2.3% for 2026. The Catalan Parliament is processing a bill that would progressively push ride-hailing out of urban transport — in progress, not in force. Either way the practical advice does not move: do not build a Barcelona airport transfer around an app. The rank always works, which is what a rank is for.

¿Es tarifa fija al aeropuerto? — es tah-REE-fah FEE-khah — is it the fixed airport fare?
¿Me pone el taxímetro, por favor? — el tak-SEE-meh-troh — could you put the meter on, please?

The hire counter, in the order it happens

One: decide about insurance before you travel, buy it in writing, and land with it printed — a screenshot on a phone at 11pm with 3% battery is not a document. Two: 'No quiero seguro adicional, gracias. Ya tengo cobertura.' Three, when he pushes back, and he probably will: '¿Me lo puede dar por escrito, por favor?' That is the emergency button, and it works because the pressure at that counter is verbal by design — almost nobody will write down a claim they cannot stand behind. Four: '¿Cuánto me bloquean en la tarjeta? ¿Cuándo me lo devuelven?' Five: '¿Es lleno a lleno?' Six, and it is not a sentence at all: video the whole car before you leave the forecourt, ending on the dashboard so the fuel gauge and the mileage are in the same shot. Spain's consumer organisation OCU and the national press documented cases through 2025 and 2026 of desks insisting that pre-bought excess cover was insufficient, with cars withheld until customers bought the in-house product; the named cases include Drivalia in Málaga and OK Mobility in Alicante. Airport counters are the most aggressive, and it is not bad luck: high turnover, short interaction, tired customer.

Ya tengo cobertura contratada. Aquí está la póliza. — yah TEN-goh koh-behr-TOO-rah — I already have cover. Here's the policy.
Prefiero no firmar hasta leerlo. — preh-FYEH-roh noh feer-MAR — I'd rather not sign until I've read it.

'Me he dejado', and the slip that makes it work

Spanish has a clean way of saying you left something behind, and it is not the word for theft. 'Me he dejado el móvil en el taxi' says the thing is where you put it and nobody did anything wrong — which is exactly the sentence that makes a driver, a barman or a receptionist go and look. 'Me han robado' says something else entirely and starts a different conversation, usually a defensive one, and it is the sentence for the police station rather than the lost jacket. The frame takes an object and a place: el móvil, la chaqueta, la maleta, las llaves, el pasaporte, followed by 'en el taxi', 'en el tren', 'aquí'. It comes back properly on the last morning of the trip. The reason to ask for a receipt on every taxi journey is that it is your only record of which car you were in, at what time, for how much: without it, a phone left in a back seat in Madrid at midnight is simply gone. It is also what any complaint about a fare needs, and it costs you four words to get.

Me he dejado la chaqueta en el taxi. — meh eh deh-KHAH-dhoh lah chah-KEH-tah — I've left my jacket in the taxi.
¿Lo han encontrado? — loh an en-kon-TRAH-dhoh — has it turned up?

Why 'me puede' and not something longer

'¿Me puede…?' is 'can you, for me' — the 'usted' form, which is the right register for a driver or a counter you have never met, and it is the same three words you met in the repair kit as '¿me lo puede escribir?'. Everything you want somebody else to do goes on the end in the dictionary form: abrir el maletero, dar por escrito, llamar un taxi, avisar en cocina, dar la factura. Two habits are worth keeping from tonight. The first is that 'por escrito' — two words — is the most powerful thing in this part, and it works at a hotel desk, a repair shop and a tour operator as well as it works at a hire counter. The second is silence: say the sentence, keep your face friendly, and do not fill the pause. The pause is doing the work.

¿Me puede llamar un taxi, por favor? — meh PWEH-dheh yah-MAR — could you call me a taxi, please?
¿Me lo puede dar por escrito? — por ehs-KREE-toh — could you give me that in writing?
