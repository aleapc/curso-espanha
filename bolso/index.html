💡 Useful to know

  • 💧 Tap water is free by law in every bar and restaurant — ask for "una jarra de agua del grifo". Refusing to serve it is not allowed.
  • ☕ The same drink can cost three different prices at the bar, at a table and on the terrace. That is legal, but the price list has to be posted — read it before you sit down outside.
  • 💳 If a card machine or cash machine offers to charge you in pounds or dollars, refuse and choose euros. That conversion typically adds around 10–13% on top.
  • 💶 Cards work almost everywhere in cities, but no business is obliged to take one. Carry €40–60 in small notes — a €50 note in a €3 bar is routinely refused.
  • 🧾 Waiters here are salaried, so tipping is extra, never the wage. Rounding up is the whole system and leaving nothing is not rude. If the card machine suggests a tip, it is voluntary — look for "Otra cantidad" or "Sin propina".
  • 🕐 Lunch typically runs 14:00–15:30 and dinner rarely starts before 21:00; kitchens close in between. At 19:00 you are not having dinner, you are having tapas — and that is a perfectly good plan.
  • 👜 The risk to you is pickpocketing, not violence, and it happens in daylight in crowds — on the metro, at busy sights. Bag in front on the train, phone off the terrace table, bag never on the back of your chair.
  • 🚔 Real Spanish police never ask to see your wallet. If someone in plain clothes shows a badge and asks for it, keep it and say "Vamos a la comisaría" — the real officer agrees, the fake one leaves.
  • 💊 Pharmacists here triage and advise at no charge — sunburn, sore throat, bites, upset stomach. If the pharmacy is shut, read the door: it names the "farmacia de guardia" that is open tonight.
  • 🏨 Some regions charge a tourist tax per person per night that is not in your online booking price. It is collected at the hotel desk, sometimes in cash. It is a real tax, not a scam.
